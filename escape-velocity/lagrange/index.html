Each dot is a spacecraft that has occupied a halo or Lissajous orbit about a Lagrange point. The view is a rotating Sun–Earth frame, with the Sun held off to the left, so Sun–Earth L1 and L2 stay in place. Distances are to scale. Earth and the Moon are drawn larger than scale in wide views so they remain visible, and they approach their true size as you zoom in. Earth–Moon L1 and L2 travel with the Moon. Times of little activity are shortened in the animation; the date shown is the real date.

Paths for most missions are JPL Horizons state vectors transformed into this rotating frame. Where Horizons does not cover a flight, or covers only part of it, the missing portion is modeled from published dates and typical halo amplitudes and is marked on that spacecraft’s information card. Occupation dates follow Jonathan McDowell’s General Catalog of Artificial Space Objects and agency mission reports.
